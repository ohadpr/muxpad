Round 3 — make it mean something

Rounds 1 and 2 were both abstract shapes that move. With width to spend, a third option opens that neither had: the indicator can stop being decorative and become a readout — something whose motion is caused by what the agent is doing rather than by a timer. Number 01 is that idea. The rest are the maximalist swings.

Widths here run 30–64px against today's 26px. Zoom 3× and Slow motion are on the bar.

01Telemetry

A 64px window onto a trace sliding past a bright head, fading out at the left edge. It reads as a strip chart because it is one — and that is the point: this one can be driven by something real. Swap the pre-generated path for a rolling buffer of tokens-per-second, or a tick per tool call, and the indicator stops saying "something is happening" and starts saying what, and how fast.

Spiky means a burst of tool calls; flat means a long silent model call; the head stalling is a stall. You would be able to tell a wedged turn from a thinking one without reading anything, which is the thing no spinner anywhere can do. As drawn here it is pre-generated and costs one CSS translate — the real version costs a path rewrite per second, which is nothing.

02Split-flap

A departure board, mid-flip, forever. Four drums rolling glyphs behind a hairline seam at the fold, each at its own speed so they never land together. Mechanical and tactile in a way no circle is — and it rhymes with the fact that muxpad is a terminal cockpit, not a chat app with a terminal in it.

The obvious next move: let them settle on the tool's actual name, then roll again when the tool changes.

03Warp

Streaks accelerating out of a point and stretching as they go — the width is animated, so each one elongates into its own motion blur exactly the way a long exposure does. Seven of them on offset delays, so the field never pulses in unison.

The only one here with a sense of speed rather than effort.

04Chaos

A real double pendulum, integrated every frame, dragging a decaying trail. Not quasi-periodic like the epicycles — genuinely chaotic: it is sensitive to initial conditions, so two of them diverge exponentially and no run is ever repeatable, even in principle.

It is hypnotic, and it is the single most likely thing on this page to get someone to ask what it is. It needs a physics step per frame, which at 30px is absurd and I enjoy that.

05Braille wave

A sine wave rendered in Unicode braille cells, scrolling. Braille is a 2×4 dot matrix per character, so a line of it is a 4-pixel-tall bitmap display made of text — which is how terminal plotting libraries draw graphs, and it is the most muxpad-native idea here.

It inherits the chat font, scales with text, and copies as characters.

06Helix

Two counter-phase dot trains crossing, each dot scaling and fading as it passes through the far side — flat circles reading as a strand twisting in depth. It travels left to right, so unlike everything circular it has a direction, which suits a thing that is getting somewhere.

07Inchworm

One pill. It anchors its tail, stretches its head forward, then pulls its tail up to meet it — and loops. A single element with four keyframes, and it is the only thing in three rounds with a personality rather than a mechanism.

The risk is exactly that: it is cute, and cute wears out faster than quiet.

Where I actually land

01 Telemetry, and it is not close. Everything in three rounds has been a shape that moves because a timer says so — this is the only one where the motion could be caused by the work. A trace fed by tokens-per-second and tool-call ticks would let you tell a bursty turn from a long silent model call from a wedged one, from across the room, with nothing to read. That is a genuinely new capability wearing an animation's clothes, and it costs one CSS translate until you decide to feed it.

For pure spectacle: 04 Chaos. A double pendulum at 30px is a ridiculous thing to ship and the only honest answer to "crazier" — it is not quasi-periodic like the epicycles, it is actually chaotic, and two of them diverge exponentially rather than merely drifting.

05 Braille is the one most native to what muxpad is, and it is nearly free. 02 Split-flap is the most fun that could also carry information — let the drums settle on the tool's name. If you want none of this, round 2's composer halo is still the answer that costs no pixels at all.
