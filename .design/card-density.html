Reveal — and the thing it cannot do

At rest, pure content plus a 3px rail. It is the densest honest option and it reads beautifully on a desktop: hover a card below and the name and age fade in.

On a phone there is no hover, so the reveal has to be a tap — which is already the collapse gesture. Three cards become three anonymous lines, and the only way to tell them apart is to read their contents. Fine for one card, actively bad for four. The weld — a pinned line, not a slab

Take B's rail and B's refusal to draw a box, and put A's name on the row instead of behind a hover. What is left is not a card at all: it is a line with a coloured edge, and nothing is hidden behind a gesture — name, value and health are all on screen, on every device, at one row each.

The border, the background slab and the shadow all go. They were doing one job — "this is pinned, not a message" — and the rail does that job in 2.5px of width instead of 34px of height. The age returns only when the writer stops, in amber, which is the one moment it is worth a glance.

Cost: it is quieter, so a card stops announcing itself. If a card is something you want unmissable rather than merely present, this is the wrong direction — and it leans harder on the agent writing a tight one-line value, because there is no chrome left to hide behind.

Two things it needs that are not cosmetic: the age must disappear until overdue (otherwise the row carries noise at the one width where width is scarce), and the content must ellipsize, not wrap — a one-row card that silently becomes a two-row card on a phone has given the whole saving back. Tapping the row expands it; that is what the collapse toggle becomes.

If you want B pure, the honest version is B on a pointer, A without one — one layout that drops the name into the row when there is no hover (@media (hover: none)). That is a real design rather than a compromise, but it is two things to keep working instead of one.
