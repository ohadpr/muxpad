Round 4 — the deep end

Three rounds of shapes, curves and readouts. This one goes after the thing none of them had: emergence — patterns that nobody drew, that come out of a rule running. Two of these are things you cannot author frame by frame even if you want to.

01Moiré

Two line gratings at 0.9px, counter-rotating at 9s and 11s. Nothing is drawn but straight lines — every swirl you see is interference, a pattern that exists in neither layer and only in their overlap. It blooms, collapses to near-solid, and blooms somewhere else, on a 99-second beat neither period has on its own — so two panes that started at different moments are showing different patterns, as above.

And it is two divs and one keyframe. No JS, no SVG, no filter, no canvas — the cheapest thing in four rounds and, at 3.2× zoom, the most alive. That ratio is the whole argument for it.

02Tesseract

A 4-dimensional hypercube, rotating in two independent planes at once — XW and YZ, which have no 3D equivalent — projected to 3D, then to the screen. The inner cube appears to turn itself inside out and swallow the outer one, because it is doing exactly that: in 4D they are the same cube seen from two sides of a rotation you cannot picture.

Sixteen vertices, thirty-two edges, two matrix multiplies per frame. Nothing else here makes you stop and work out what you are looking at.

03Whip

Twelve Verlet points with distance constraints and a little gravity, anchored at the left while the anchor itself traces a slow circle. The result is a rope that cracks — the tip accelerates far past anything driving it, because the constraint solver pumps energy down the chain exactly the way a real whip does.

No keyframe anywhere. The motion is a consequence, not a description, which is why it never looks like it is on a loop.

04Arc

Midpoint displacement, regenerated six times a second: a path is split, its middle kicked sideways by a shrinking random amount, recursively. It is the standard way to make lightning and it never produces the same bolt twice — with a soft glow pass underneath for the discharge.

The most aggressive thing in four rounds. Which may be wrong for "please wait".

05Phyllotaxis

Fifty-five dots placed at the golden angle, 137.5°, which is how a sunflower packs seeds and the only angle that never lines up into spokes. A brightness wave runs outward through them — and because the spiral arms are emergent, not drawn, the wave appears to travel along arms that do not exist in the code.

Clean, mathematical, and quiet enough to live beside text.

06Flow field

Eighteen particles steered by a smooth vector field and recycled at the left edge when they drift off. Nothing is choreographed — each one just reads the field where it happens to be, so they bunch into currents and thin out into eddies on their own.

The closest thing here to watching weather.

07Decode

No indicator. The tool's own name resolves out of noise, character by character, then scrambles and resolves again as the tool changes. The thing that tells you what is happening is the thing that tells you it is still happening — round 2's idea, with teeth.

It is the only one that gets MORE informative the longer you look, and it costs a textContent write at 20 Hz.

Where I land, for the last time

01 Moiré. It is the only thing in four rounds where the striking part is not drawn by anyone — two sets of straight lines, and every swirl is interference between them. It beats on a 99-second cycle, so it does not repeat in any sitting; two panes are visibly different within seconds; and it is two divs, one keyframe, no JS, no SVG, no filter. Cheapest and wildest at the same time, which does not usually happen.

02 Tesseract is the one people will ask about. 03 Whip is the one that feels alive. 07 Decode is the one that is actually useful, and the only one here I would ship alongside rather than instead of something.

Honest note: 04 Arc is the coolest-looking and the most wrong — lightning says something went bang, and this element's whole job is to say be patient.
