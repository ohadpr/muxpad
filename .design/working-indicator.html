Round 2 — stop making a spinner

Round 1 was nine variations on a small thing moving in a loop. That is the whole genre, and the genre was the problem. Three different swings here: motion that is quasi-periodic (no common period, so it never reads as a loop), motion that draws (a curve being made rather than a shape being spun), and no glyph at all — the indicator becomes a property of something already on screen.

Hit Zoom 3× and Slow motion together. At 26px most of this is felt rather than read, which is the point — but you should see what you are buying.

01Epicycles

Three arms on three arms: a 3.1s wheel carrying a 1.23s wheel carrying a 0.47s wheel, with a glowing tip on the end. This is how a Fourier series draws — and because those periods share no common factor, the tip never retraces its own path. The combined cycle is minutes long. The second one above is the same animation started 1.4s later, and in a cycle minutes long that offset never comes back around — two busy panes are permanently, visibly out of step. No spinner in the world does that; two of those are always identical.

It is also quietly the right metaphor: nested rotations composing into something that looks nothing like rotation.

03Murmuration

Seven dots, seven radii, seven periods sharing no factor — 1.3s to 3.7s. They clump, smear into an arc, scatter, and gather somewhere else. There is no loop to catch: the combined period runs to minutes. It feels alive rather than mechanical, and it is seven divs and one keyframe.

09No glyph — the composer is the indicator

The biggest swing here. A light travels the composer's own border while a turn runs; at rest it is the ordinary hairline. No new element, no new space, and it is impossible to miss because it is wrapped around the thing you are already looking at.

It also answers a question the dots never could — is this pane busy? — from the corner of your eye, with nothing to read. The dots live at the bottom of the transcript and scroll away with it; the composer never moves.

What I would actually do

09 the composer halo, with 08 the shimmering label — together, and no glyph at all. They are not two options, they are one idea: the indicator stops being an object you add and becomes a property of things already on screen. The halo says this pane is working from across the room; the shimmer says and here is what it is doing when you look. Nothing is added, nothing takes space, and the row stops having a little mascot in it.

If it must be a glyph: 01 Epicycles. It is the only thing in either round that is not a loop — the periods share no common factor, so the tip never retraces its path and two working panes visibly drift apart. That is hard to un-notice once you have seen it, and it costs three nested transforms.

03 Murmuration is the same trick with a softer face, and the one I would take if the epicycle arms read as too mechanical. 07 Life is the most fun and the least shippable.
