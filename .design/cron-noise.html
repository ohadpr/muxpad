The Investing chat — what was noise, what is left, and the one call I need you to make

Three different things were stacked on top of each other in that screenshot. One was a plain bug and is fixed. One is the fold working as designed. The third is a decision, because fixing it breaks a rule I have been defending all week.

fixed

The raw <muxpad-cron> bubble was a bug

That block of XML at the bottom of your screenshot should never have existed. A fire drained from the queue renders an optimistic echo while the runner writes it to disk — and that one render path was the only place in muxpad that did not split the marker off first. The transcript does it, the queue preview does it; the echo used the raw text.

Its handler even says why it was missed: "a turn nobody started (a cron fire, a wakeup) carries no text and sets nothing." True when written, false once the queue drain started stamping the text it relays. It now goes through the same component as the queue — and for a folded fire that means nothing renders at all.

the decision

The two loud fires are older than the --fold flag

The 03:13 and 03:18 rows print their whole prompt, their tool call and their "closed" — six rows for two fires. Every newer fire of that same schedule is silent. The difference is not the schedule; it is when the message was delivered. Each fire stores a copy of the fold flag as it was at that moment, and nw-hourly was not folded yet.

I have twice told you this was correct, on the principle that muxpad renders a message as it was delivered — it tails the harness's transcript, it does not rewrite history. I still think that principle is right. But it is producing a chat where the same schedule looks like two different things, and the stored flag is a copy of a fact that lives in the crons table — which is the exact defect shape that has bitten this feature four times already.

today: 6 rows for those two fires · 1 chip + 1 fold for the other seven

AResolve fold live, from the cron

Ask the crons table whether nw-hourly is folded now, and ignore the copy frozen into each message. Turning --fold on cleans up the back-scroll; turning it off reveals it again.

The cost: the transcript stops being a photograph. A row you remember reading can change shape later. That is the rule I have been defending — and the thing being changed is only how loudly muxpad's own plumbing is drawn, never what anyone said.

BLeave history alone; collapse it by age

Keep the stored flag authoritative, but fold any cron turn older than the current session into a single quiet marker regardless of its flag — recent fires keep whatever shape they were delivered in.

The cost: two rules instead of one, and the boundary moves on its own. A fire you were reading five minutes ago looks different after a reload.

CRewrite the two stored markers, once

A one-off repair that stamps fold="1" into the two historical messages, so every fire of a folded schedule agrees. No code changes at all after that.

The cost: it edits the harness's own transcript on disk — the file muxpad is supposed to only ever read. And it fixes today, not the next schedule you fold.

The cost: nothing, if you can ignore them — but you have now raised this three times, which is the real argument against.
