The pinned card — five treatments

The shape is settled: one row, 34px, value left, age hard right. What is not settled is what it looks like — right now it is a white bar with a rounded rectangle under it, and the composer is also a white bar with rounded corners, so the two read as a pair of inputs rather than as a value and a text field.

Each is shown with the fold above and the composer below, because the relationship to the composer is the actual problem. Light first — it is the theme in use.

judge the card against the composer, not on its own

nowRail + white bar

Two stacked light bars with rounded corners. The rail is the only thing saying "this is not another input", and at 2.5px it is doing a lot of work alone.

aLedger

No surface at all — a hairline rule and monospace figures, like the last line of a terminal. It stops competing with the composer by refusing to be a box, and tabular numerals mean the digits never jitter as the value updates. The most muxpad-native of the five and the quietest.

Risk: with nothing behind it, the transcript scrolls right up to it.

bTape

A paper receipt: warm off-white, square-ish corners, and a dotted leader running from the value to the age the way a contents page does. The leader is the trick — it ties the two ends together so the gap reads as deliberate rather than as empty space.

Risk: the warm tint is a second accent colour in a purple app.

dWell

Recessed instead of raised: an inner shadow makes it a slot cut into the background, while the composer below is lifted off it. That one inversion is the whole idea — the two stop looking alike because they are on opposite sides of the surface.

My pick: A Ledger. The problem is that the card and the composer look like two of the same thing, and A is the only one that solves it by subtraction — no surface, no border, no shadow, so there is nothing left to compare. It is also the only one whose figures stop jittering when the value changes, which on a number that updates hourly is worth more than it sounds.

If you want it to look designed rather than quiet: E Signal. It is the only one that gives the content a hierarchy instead of treating it as one grey string, and that is a real improvement rather than a finish. The cost is that it needs the card's first token to be a headline — which your market card has and a freeform one would not.

D Well is the cheapest real fix if you like the current shape and only want it to stop rhyming with the composer. C Glass is the prettiest and the most expensive. B Tape is the one I would not ship: the dotted leader is lovely and the warm paper is a second accent colour this app does not have.
