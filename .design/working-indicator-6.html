Round 6 — small, and alive

I picked the moiré after judging it at 3× zoom on a dark background. Your theme is light and you see it at 1×, where 0.8px lines on a 2.3px pitch alias into a grey smudge — it reads as a rendering artifact, and it spends 120px saying nothing. That is on me; this page is light-first and at real size, with zoom as the option rather than the default.

Everything below is 20–46px instead of 120, and aims at character rather than spectacle: squash and stretch, anticipation, overshoot — the things that make a shape read as alive rather than as a loop.

01Bounce

One dot, doing the oldest trick in animation properly: it squashes on landing, stretches on the way up, hangs at the top, and its shadow spreads and fades as it rises. Nothing about that is a loop you notice — it reads as weight.

26px. Two elements. The shadow is what sells it; without it this is a dot moving up and down.

02Muncher

Chomps its way along a line of dots, which vanish as it reaches them and come back behind it. The most overtly playful thing here and the only one that is funny.

46px, so it costs width — and it is a strong flavour. Charming on day one; ask yourself about day ninety.

03Eyes

Two eyes that glance around and blink every four seconds. Nothing reads as alive faster than a blink — and the pupils moving left and right is exactly the gesture for "looking at something".

24px. The risk is that it is a face, and a face in your chat is a bigger personality decision than an animation.

04Leapfrog

Your three dots — except the back one jumps over the other two and lands in front, forever. Same furniture as today, completely different behaviour, and it reads instantly because the arc is a real arc.

06Walker

The text caret, with legs, walking on the spot — the body bobs on the off-beat the way a real gait does. It is the cursor that is about to appear anyway, which makes it the most on-brand of the six.

Shipping 01 Bounce

It is the one that is alive without being a character. The muncher and the eyes both introduce a personality that has to stay funny for months; a bouncing ball is just physics, and physics does not get old. It reads perfectly at 1× in both themes, costs 26px instead of 120, and is two elements animating nothing but transform.

The shadow is the part that matters — spreading and fading as the ball rises is what makes it read as weight rather than as a dot sliding up a line. Leapfrog is the safe second if you want to keep the three dots you know.
